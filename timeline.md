## Hitler becomes Reich Chancellor
**Date:** 30 January 1933

President Hindenburg appoints Adolf Hitler chancellor of a coalition cabinet with the conservative DNVP. Only three of its members are Nazis, and his conservative partners believe they can "tame" him. That evening, SA columns march through the Brandenburg Gate in a torchlight parade. The *Machtergreifung* has begun.

## Reichstag dissolved
**Date:** 1 February 1933

At Hitler's request, Hindenburg dissolves the Reichstag and calls new elections for 5 March. The NSDAP now campaigns with the full resources of the state behind it.

## Decree "for the Protection of the German People"
**Date:** 4 February 1933

An emergency decree lets the government ban newspapers and political meetings. It is used immediately against the Social Democratic and Communist press during the election campaign.

## SA and SS become auxiliary police
**Date:** 22 February 1933

As Prussian interior minister, Hermann Göring enlists around 50,000 men from the SA, SS and Stahlhelm as *Hilfspolizei*. Party thugs can now terrorise political opponents with the authority of the state.

## The Reichstag fire
**Date:** 27 February 1933

The parliament building in Berlin burns. The Dutch communist Marinus van der Lubbe is arrested at the scene. The Nazi leadership immediately calls it the signal for a communist uprising.

## Reichstag Fire Decree
**Date:** 28 February 1933

The *Verordnung zum Schutz von Volk und Staat* suspends basic rights, including freedom of speech, of the press and of assembly, the privacy of mail, and protection from arbitrary arrest. Thousands of Communists are taken into "protective custody". The decree stays in force until 1945.

## The last multi-party Reichstag election
**Date:** 5 March 1933

Despite terror and censorship, the NSDAP wins only 43.9% of the vote. With its DNVP allies it has a narrow majority. In the days that follow, Nazi Reich commissioners take over the state governments that are not yet under Nazi control.

## A ministry for propaganda
**Date:** 13 March 1933

Joseph Goebbels becomes head of the new Reich Ministry for Public Enlightenment and Propaganda. It will steer the press, radio, film and the arts.

## Day of Potsdam
**Date:** 21 March 1933

The new Reichstag opens in Potsdam's Garrison Church with a staged handshake between Hitler and Hindenburg, meant to show that the "new" Germany continues the old Prussian traditions. On the same day, the *Heimtückeverordnung* makes criticism of the government a crime.

## Dachau opens
**Date:** 22 March 1933

The first prisoners, most of them political opponents, arrive at the new concentration camp near Munich, which Heinrich Himmler had announced two days earlier. Dachau becomes the model for the camp system.

## The Enabling Act
**Date:** 23 March 1933

With SA men lining the hall, the Reichstag votes 444 to 94 to give the government power to pass laws without parliament, even ones that deviate from the constitution. The Communist deputies have been arrested or have fled, and only the SPD votes against. Otto Wels speaks for them: "You can take our lives and our freedom, but not our honour."

## The states are "coordinated"
**Date:** 31 March 1933

The first law bearing the word *Gleichschaltung* dissolves the state parliaments and re-forms them according to the Reichstag results, without the Communists. A second law a week later installs *Reichsstatthalter* (Reich governors) above the state governments.

## Boycott of Jewish businesses
**Date:** 1 April 1933

SA men post themselves outside Jewish-owned shops, law offices and doctors' practices and turn customers away. It is the first nationwide action against Germany's Jews organised by the state.

## Civil service purged
**Date:** 7 April 1933

The Law for the Restoration of the Professional Civil Service lets the regime dismiss officials who are "politically unreliable". Its "Aryan paragraph" forces out civil servants of Jewish descent, and professional associations soon copy the rule.

## The Gestapo is founded
**Date:** 26 April 1933

Göring creates the Secret State Police Office in Prussia. The political police can now arrest people and send them to camps without any court review.

## Trade unions smashed
**Date:** 2 May 1933

One day after the regime staged 1 May as the "Day of National Labour", SA and SS men occupy the free trade unions' offices, seize their assets and arrest their leaders. Workers are forced into the German Labour Front (DAF) under Robert Ley.

## Book burnings
**Date:** 10 May 1933

In Berlin's Opernplatz and in university towns across Germany, students burn books by Jewish, pacifist, socialist and other "un-German" authors, among them Heinrich Heine, Erich Kästner, Karl Marx and Sigmund Freud.

## SPD banned
**Date:** 22 June 1933

Interior Minister Wilhelm Frick bans the Social Democratic Party as "hostile to the state and the people". Its deputies lose their seats, and many of its members are arrested.

## The remaining parties dissolve themselves
**Date:** 27 June – 5 July 1933

Under pressure, the DNVP, the liberal DStP and DVP, the Bavarian People's Party and finally the Catholic Centre Party dissolve themselves one after another. Only the NSDAP is left.

## One-party state by law
**Date:** 14 July 1933

The Law against the Formation of New Parties makes the NSDAP the only legal party in Germany. On the same day, the government passes a law allowing the forced sterilisation of people deemed "hereditarily ill".

## Concordat with the Vatican
**Date:** 20 July 1933

The Holy See and the Reich sign the *Reichskonkordat*. In return for guarantees for church life, Catholic clergy withdraw from politics. For the regime it is a major gain in international prestige.

## Reich Chamber of Culture
**Date:** 22 September 1933

Anyone working in literature, music, theatre, film, the fine arts, the press or radio now has to belong to one of Goebbels' chambers. Exclusion amounts to a ban on working in one's profession.

## Editors' Law
**Date:** 4 October 1933

The *Schriftleitergesetz* makes newspaper editors answerable to the state rather than to their publishers. Jews and anyone deemed politically unreliable are barred from journalism.

## Germany leaves the League of Nations
**Date:** 14 October 1933

Hitler withdraws Germany from the Geneva Disarmament Conference and the League of Nations, and dissolves the Reichstag once more.

## Single-list "election"
**Date:** 12 November 1933

Voters can only approve or reject a single NSDAP list, which receives about 92%. A referendum held alongside it, on leaving the League of Nations, returns about 95% approval.

## The states are abolished in all but name
**Date:** 30 January 1934

On the first anniversary of the Machtergreifung, the Law on the Reconstruction of the Reich abolishes the state parliaments and transfers the states' sovereign rights to the Reich. Two weeks later, the Reichsrat is abolished as well.

## Night of the Long Knives
**Date:** 30 June – 2 July 1934

Hitler has SA chief Ernst Röhm and the SA leadership murdered, alongside conservative critics and old rivals such as Kurt von Schleicher and Gregor Strasser. At least 90 people are killed. The army welcomes the removal of its SA rival.

## Murder declared legal
**Date:** 3 July 1934

A one-sentence law declares the killings "legal as acts of self-defence of the state". The state's leaders now openly stand above the law.

## Hindenburg dies. Hitler becomes "Führer"
**Date:** 2 August 1934

Under a law passed the day before, the offices of Reich President and Chancellor are merged when Hindenburg dies. Hitler becomes *Führer und Reichskanzler*. The same day, soldiers swear an oath of loyalty to him personally.

## The plebiscite
**Date:** 19 August 1934

A referendum approves the merger of the offices with about 90% of the vote. Nineteen months after Hitler's appointment, the Machtergreifung is complete, and nothing remains of the Weimar Republic's checks on power.
